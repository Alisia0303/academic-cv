## About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Washington State University, working on theoretically grounded and computationally efficient methods for continual learning in deep neural networks.

My research investigates how pretrained models can continuously acquire, retain, and retrieve knowledge under non-stationary data distributions. It combines parameter-efficient adaptation, probabilistic modeling, and statistical analysis to develop scalable learning and memory mechanisms for deep networks and autonomous agents.

My current work extends these ideas toward continual generative memory, scalable adapter retrieval, and decentralized knowledge alignment across LLM agents.

Before my doctoral studies, I gained over three years of industry experience developing production machine learning systems and large-scale data pipelines at Momo, VNG, and Zalo.

## Selected Research

### Task-Free Continual Learning (HESTIA)

*Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence (UAI), 2026*

Developed an order-invariant linearized parameter-efficient fine-tuning approach using closed-form ridge updates over accumulated sufficient statistics. Combined with density-guided adapter routing, this framework accommodates non-stationary distribution shifts while reducing catastrophic forgetting by 5–7× compared with strong baselines.

### Continual Fine-Tuning with Parameter-Free Task Retrieval (PROTEUS)

*arXiv Preprint, 2026*

Developed probabilistic DP-GMM signatures for retrieving pretrained adaptation modules without additional learned routing parameters. Established an exponential retrieval-error bound and achieved up to 57% improvement in retrieval accuracy over previous retrieval-based approaches across six class-incremental benchmarks.

### Continual Memory for LLM Agents (Ongoing Research)

Investigating signature-indexed parametric memory for continual knowledge acquisition in LLM agents, including budgeted consolidation, compact generative representations, and sublinear routing. Related work explores probabilistic frameworks for decentralized skill alignment and knowledge sharing across heterogeneous agents.
